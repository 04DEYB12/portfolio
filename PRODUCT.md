## Platform

web

## Users

Two audiences, weighted equally:

- **Recruiters and employers** evaluating Dave for an entry-level software development role.
- **Potential clients** evaluating Dave for freelance or contract work.

Visitors arrive via links shared on resumes, LinkedIn, GitHub, or direct referral. They scan quickly and decide whether to reach out.

## Product Purpose

A personal portfolio that proves Dave can ship real software. It showcases his full-stack PHP/MySQL projects, skills, education, credentials, and GitHub activity, and converts visitors into contact (email, LinkedIn, or GitHub).

Success means visitors understand what Dave builds and trust him enough to start a conversation — whether that conversation is a job offer or a freelance engagement.

## Positioning

A working full-stack developer, not just a student: every showcased project is a database-driven system (clinic management, POS/kiosk, internal database tools, faculty evaluation) built with PHP, MySQL, HTML/CSS, JavaScript, and Tailwind. The portfolio's claim is practical, deployed-style systems work rather than toy demos.

## Product Principles

1. Show real systems, not aspirations — every claim maps to a project, credential, or verifiable profile.
2. Serve both audiences without splitting the page: one portfolio should make Dave hireable and hireable-as-a-contractor.
3. Proof over adjectives — credentials, contribution activity, and project specifics carry the argument.
4. Earn contact through clarity: a visitor should know what Dave builds and how to reach him within one scroll.
5. Keep the site honest to his stage — entry-level, learning in public, no inflated claims.
